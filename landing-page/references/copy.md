# Landing page copy

Generic copy comes from skipping research. Mine real customer language, state a concrete
outcome, never invent a claim.

## Workflow

1. **Customer language first.** Collect verbatim phrases from reviews (low-star ones teach
   most), support tickets, sales-call notes, community threads or the user's own notes. Sort
   into pain, desire, objection, proof. The most frequent pain becomes the hero, in the
   customer's words. If there is no research, say so and ask for a few real quotes or write
   from the brief and mark the copy as unvalidated.
2. **Pick a frame by audience awareness.** Cold or unaware: lead with the outcome (AIDA).
   Problem-aware: name the pain, then the fix (PAS). Skeptical, high-consideration buyers:
   spend more space on proof and objections. One page serves one awareness level; segment
   rather than average.
3. **Draft per block**, 3 to 5 variants for headline, subhead, bullets, CTA. Choose, don't
   merge.
4. **Run the line filter** below on every line.
5. **Hand off** to `natural-writing` for rhythm and stock-phrase tells.

## Headline and subhead

- Write the headline last, after the offer and proof are settled, so it states the
  strongest claim you can support.
- Shape: a specific outcome for a specific audience, with the pain or timeframe attached.
  Statements beat questions. Roughly 4 to 11 words.
- Avoid "The [adjective] [category] for [audience]" and abstract slogans that fit any
  competitor.
- The first screen answers: what is this, who is it for, why trust it, what do I do next.
  The subhead adds the one fact the headline can't carry: how, or for whom.
- Turn each feature into a benefit ("so that"). Replace vague adjectives with facts:
  integrations by name, setup time in minutes.

## CTA labels

- Describe the result of the click: "Start my free trial", "Get the pricing PDF". Not
  "Submit" or "Learn more".
- Match commitment to traffic temperature: soft asks for cold traffic, trial or demo for
  warm, purchase for hot. Do not ask cold traffic to "Request a demo".
- Put reassurance microcopy under the final button ("No card required") only if it is true.

## Proof

- Specific beats generic: name, role, number, outcome. "Trusted by thousands" and bare star
  counts read as fake.
- At least one proof element on the first screen. Place each next to the claim or objection
  it supports: results near the headline, ROI near pricing, security near the form.
- 3 to 5 distinct proof types at most. Never a footer-only proof block.
- No proof available: use a labeled placeholder, never an invention.

## Objections

Add a block near the CTA that answers the likely doubts: price, switching cost, security,
"will it work for me", time to value. Use the customer's own objections from step 1, not
generic ones.

## Mobile

Review copy as a separate pass. Trim about 15 to 20 percent of AI-drafted text. The headline
and the primary CTA must fit on a 390px-wide first screen.

## Line filter

For every line ask:
- Could this appear unchanged on a competitor's page? Cut or make it specific.
- Does it add information the previous line did not? Cut.
- Is it a claim? Where is the evidence on the page?
- Is the message consistent with the ad, link or search that brought the visitor here
  (message match)? If the source is unknown, ask.
